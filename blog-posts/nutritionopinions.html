8 Nutrition Opinions I Stand By (Even If the Internet Doesn't Agree)

August 9, 2026 12 min read Nutrition Opinions, Circadian Rhythm, Fibre, Gut Health, Stress, Carbohydrates, Lifestyle

Table of Contents

    Nutrition isn't about chasing trends. It's about respecting human physiology. Discover eight opinions backed by science and clinical practice.

    Nutrition is one of those fields where everyone has an opinion. Some of mine are backed by physiology, some by years of clinical practice, and some by watching the same patterns repeat themselves in hundreds of clients.

    You don't have to agree with every one of them.

    But if you understand the biology behind them, you'll understand why I keep repeating these in almost every consultation.

    1. You Cannot Out-Eat a Disrupted Circadian Rhythm

    People spend hours perfecting their diet but never ask themselves one simple question:

    "Did my brain see the sun today?"

    Morning sunlight is one of the strongest signals that sets your circadian rhythm. Light entering the eyes resets the suprachiasmatic nucleus—the brain's master clock—which coordinates the release of cortisol in the morning and melatonin at night. That rhythm influences sleep quality, appetite, insulin sensitivity, mood and even immune function.

    You can eat the cleanest diet in the world, but if you're sleeping at 2 a.m., waking up late and spending your entire day indoors, your physiology is constantly working against you. Food cannot completely compensate for a disrupted biological clock.

    2. Antibiotic Resistance Is One of the Biggest Health Threats We'll Face

    Antibiotics have saved millions of lives.

    The problem isn't antibiotics.

    The problem is how casually we've started using them.

    Every unnecessary course, every incomplete prescription and every inappropriate use gives bacteria another opportunity to adapt and survive. Those resistant bacteria don't care whether the antibiotic came from a doctor's prescription, livestock farming or environmental contamination—they simply evolve.

    Use antibiotics when they're genuinely needed. Complete the prescribed course. Protect one of modern medicine's greatest discoveries instead of slowly making it ineffective.

    3. Fibre Deserves the Same Attention as Protein

    Protein builds you.

    Fibre feeds the ecosystem that helps maintain you.

    Your gut microbiome depends on fibre to produce short-chain fatty acids like butyrate, acetate and propionate. These compounds nourish the cells lining your intestine, strengthen the gut barrier, regulate immune function and influence glucose metabolism.

    We've become excellent at counting grams of protein while forgetting that trillions of bacteria also need to be fed. A healthy microbiome isn't built by supplements alone—it's built by consistently eating fibre-rich foods.

    4. Mental Stress Always Becomes Physical Stress

    Stress doesn't stay in your head.

    Your body experiences every bit of it.

    When you're under chronic stress, your sympathetic nervous system—the fight-or-flight response—becomes more active. Blood flow is redirected away from digestion towards muscles and the brain. Digestive enzyme secretion changes, stomach emptying can slow or speed up, intestinal muscle contractions become irregular and communication along the gut-brain axis becomes disrupted.

    That's why stress often shows up as bloating, acid reflux, burping, constipation, diarrhoea, irregular bowel movements or appetite changes. Your gut isn't "overreacting." It's responding to the nervous system that's controlling it.

    5. Carbohydrates Were Never the Villain

    The real question has never been,

    "Should I eat carbohydrates?"

    It's,

    "Which carbohydrates am I choosing most of the time?"

    Your body doesn't respond to an apple the same way it responds to a sugary soft drink. A bowl of rice with dal, vegetables and curd behaves very differently from a burger, fries and a milkshake. Fibre, protein, food structure, processing and meal composition all influence digestion, glucose absorption and satiety.

    Learning to distinguish between minimally processed carbohydrates and ultra-processed foods is far more useful than fearing carbohydrates altogether.

    6. Lifestyle Diseases Are Exactly That—Lifestyle Diseases

    Food matters. But health isn't built only when you're eating.

    It's built while you're sleeping, walking, exercising, recovering, spending time outdoors, maintaining relationships and managing stress.

    Movement improves insulin sensitivity. Sleep regulates appetite hormones and glucose metabolism. Exercise preserves muscle mass. Social connection lowers chronic stress. Recovery allows the body to repair itself.

    Health isn't created during one workout. It's created during the other twenty-three hours of the day.

    7. Feed Your Gut Before You Buy a Probiotic

    Before buying the latest probiotic supplement, ask yourself a simpler question. Have I actually fed my existing bacteria? Your gut microbes need fibre, resistant starch, legumes, whole grains, fruits, vegetables and plant diversity to thrive. Without food, even beneficial bacteria struggle to survive.

    Supplements can have their place, but they should never replace the daily nutrition your microbiome depends on.

    8. Food Is More Than Calories and Protein

    This is probably my strongest nutrition opinion.

    Food isn't just carbohydrates, proteins and fats.

    It's taste. Texture. Temperature. Culture. Timing. Memory. Satiety. Digestion.

    Even the way food feels in your mouth changes how your brain responds to it. Crunchy foods increase chewing, slowing eating and allowing more time for satiety hormones to develop. Creamy and umami-rich foods often increase satisfaction because they activate reward pathways linked to protein-rich foods. Warm meals tend to empty from the stomach differently than ice-cold foods and often feel more comforting because they engage both physiological and psychological responses.

    Taste matters too. Sweet, sour, salty, bitter, umami and even fat as an emerging taste all activate different sensory pathways before digestion has even begun. A meal with diverse tastes often naturally encourages a wider variety of foods, making it easier to include carbohydrates, proteins, healthy fats, fibre and phytonutrients without obsessively counting nutrients.

    Nutrition begins long before absorption. It starts the moment your senses experience food.

    My Biggest Nutrition Opinion?

    Health isn't built by chasing the next superfood, supplement or social media trend.

    It's built by respecting human physiology.

    Sleep with the sun. Move every day. Feed your microbiome. Manage stress. Eat real food. And remember that your body isn't simply counting calories. It's responding to every signal you give it.
